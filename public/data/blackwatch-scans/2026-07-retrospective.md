### Overconfidence Patterns
1. **Escalation bias:** The system consistently assigned higher probability to escalation scenarios (Hormuz closure, oil to $120+) than materialized. The base rate of de-escalation/pause was underestimated throughout.

### Recommended Process Adjustments

1. **Base rate anchoring:** Before stating scenario probabilities, explicitly check: "What is the historical base rate of [X] happening?" This would have prevented the consistent over-weighting of escalation scenarios.
2. **De-escalation scenarios as primary, not inverse:** The system treated de-escalation as the "inverse scenario" of escalation. In future, treat both as primary scenarios with independent probability assessments.
3. **Proxy indicator audit:** Monthly review of proxy indicators to check if the proxy relationship still holds. Oil→supply chain, VIX→geopolitics, consumer sentiment→social cohesion are all unvalidated proxies.
4. **Pause/ceasefire modeling:** Add explicit "pause" as a scenario type. July showed that wars can pause without resolving. The system modeled "war continues" vs "war escalates" but not "war pauses" as a distinct state.
5. **Internal politics tracking:** Add US domestic politics (Congressional pressure, Cabinet dissent) as a tracked variable. Vance/Caine opposition was a key variable in the Jul 25 pause that was completely off the radar.

## 9. KEY LESSONS FOR AUGUST 2026

1. **Wars pause more often than they escalate.** The base rate of de-escalation was consistently underestimated. August modeling should give equal weight to pause/ceasefire scenarios.

2. **Proxy indicators create false convergence.** Using Brent oil for 3 signals (energy, supply chain, Middle East) amplified single-source movements into apparent multi-domain convergence. August must add direct measures for supply chain (Drewry WCI) and geopolitics (GDELT).

3. **Markets can decouple from geopolitical risk for extended periods.** S&P 500 stayed at 7,400-7,500 despite active war, Hormuz disruption, and oil at $90+. The "VIX catch-up" scenario was wrong. Equity markets priced in conflict containment while commodity markets priced in escalation.

4. **Regulatory shifts happen during crisis coverage.** Gold Eagle (Jul 14), pharma tariffs (Jul 31), and AI Kill Switch Act all emerged while media was focused on Iran. August must explicitly scan for "quiet regulatory shifts" as a category.

5. **Data staleness is a persistent vulnerability.** Consumer sentiment (May 2026, 2 months stale), sovereign debt (Jan 2026, 6 months stale), and China CLI (2024 vintage) all created assessment lag. The system must either find fresher sources or explicitly discount stale data more aggressively.

6. **Internal political dynamics matter as much as geopolitical ones.** Vance and Caine's opposition to escalation was the key variable in the Jul 25 pause. This was completely off the radar. August must add US domestic political tracking.

7. **Weak signals can graduate faster than expected.** Houthi blockade, El Niño, and AI agent breach all went from weak signal to active risk within 1-2 weeks. The system should track weak signal "aging" and upgrade faster.

8. **Deep scans outperform echo scans.** The two deep scans (7.8 and 8.2 RIS) significantly outperformed the average echo scan (7.2). The deeper analysis, prediction tracking, and memory evolution sections of deep scans create genuine learning loops.
