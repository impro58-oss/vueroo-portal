Data Poisoning Defense Protocol

Protocol ID: eps-002

Status: Active

Scope: All research, all claims, all professional output. Especially MedTech, clinical data, competitive intelligence, and any evaluation/assessment.

Created: 2026-08-08

Updated: 2026-08-08 (v1.1 — added mandatory methodology cross-reference)

Trigger: Any time data is gathered, claims are stated, or evaluations are made.

The Problem

Data poisoning is real and accelerating. AI-generated papers with fabricated data, citation chains from compromised sources, synthetic clinical trials, manipulated statistics, and LLM hallucinations presented as fact are all increasing. In MedTech specifically, a single poisoned source can cascade through evaluations, strategy memos, and investor presentations — destroying credibility and potentially patient safety.

Roo's directive (2026-08-08): When doing research, get separate points of confirmation for data we're claiming and stating. If data poisoning is suspected or confirmed in a source, consider that source completely unusable — not partially credible, not "probably fine," but DISCARDED.

Rules

Rule 1: Independent Confirmation Required

Every factual claim requires at least 2 independent confirmation points before being stated as fact.

  • "Independent" means: different authors, different institutions, different data collection methods, or different primary sources.
  • Two articles citing the same press release = 1 confirmation point, not 2.
  • A web search result and a peer-reviewed paper confirming the same number = 2 confirmation points.

Minimum confirmation tiers:

  • MedTech clinical data: 3 points (trial results, market share, device specs)
  • Financial figures: 2 points (revenue, market size, growth rates)
  • Competitive intelligence: 2 points (product launches, regulatory status)
  • General knowledge: 1 point with vintage tag (established facts, public records)
  • Evaluations/assessments: 2+ supporting data points per conclusion (strategy recs, risk ratings)

Rule 2: Source Contamination = Total Discard

If a source is identified as poisoned (fabricated data, AI-generated papers, manipulated statistics, retracted studies, or undisclosed conflicts of interest), the entire source is unusable. Not "probably fine for non-critical claims." Unusable.

Actions on contamination detection:

  1. Flag the source with [POISONED] tag
  1. Trace all claims that relied on that source
  1. Re-confirm each affected claim through independent sources
  1. If independent confirmation doesn't exist → claim becomes [UNVERIFIED], not "probably true"
  1. Log the contamination event in the source's metadata

Rule 3: Provenance Tracking

Every data point carries its chain of custody:

[Claim] (source: [original] | confirmed-by: [2nd source, 3rd source] | vintage: [date] | confidence: [H/M/L] | poisoned: [yes/no/unknown])

If poisoned: unknown — the claim has not been verified against contamination. Treat as lower confidence.

Rule 4: Special Scrutiny Domains

These domains get heightened verification because poisoning risk and consequence are both high:

  • MedTech clinical data — AI-generated papers, fabricated trial results, ghost-authored studies
  • Regulatory claims — FDA CE marks, trial statuses, approval dates (check primary sources)
  • Market sizing — numbers that cascade through investor presentations
  • Competitive intelligence — product claims from company marketing vs. independent verification
  • Pricing — company-listed prices, ASP data, reimbursement rates
  • Any AI-generated research summary — including my own output from NotebookLM, web searches, or LLM reasoning
  • NotebookLM / LLM extractions — what was SAID, not what is TRUE. Requires independent confirmation.

Rule 5: Confirmation Methods (Ranked by Reliability)

  1. Primary source verification — clinical trial registries, regulatory databases (FDA, CE), company SEC filings, published peer-reviewed papers
  1. Cross-reference against independent databases — PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, GBD, WHO, Eurostat
  1. Live web search confirmation — find the same claim reported by independent outlets
  1. Expert / VOT confirmation — confirmed by someone with direct knowledge
  1. Single source with strong provenance — peer-reviewed, reputable institution, recent vintage

Never acceptable as sole confirmation: LLM output, AI summaries, blog posts without primary source links, social media posts, press releases without supporting data.

Rule 6: Evaluation Integrity

When making evaluations (strategy recommendations, risk assessments, competitive ratings):

  • State the data sources behind each evaluation point
  • If a source cannot be independently confirmed, the evaluation point is marked [UNCONFIRMED]
  • Evaluations do NOT get a free pass because they're "subjective" — every assessment must trace to verifiable data
  • When in doubt, downgrade the confidence level. NEVER upgrade.

Mandatory Methodology Cross-Reference

When doing any evaluation, pricing, market sizing, or opportunity assessment, the correct methodology protocol MUST be activated alongside EPS-002. Data poisoning defense without proper structure is just caution — structure without verification is just process. Both together produce reliable output.

Task → Protocol Mapping:

  • Market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM): RSC-009 (Market Sizing & TAM Analysis). Every dollar figure gets confirmation points. No single-source market claims.
  • Market research: RSC-004 (Market Research Protocol). Every number tagged [VERIFIED/ESTIMATE/0.1 MARKER]. Harvard citations mandatory.
  • Pricing: RSC-009 (bottom-up unit economics) + competitive pricing confirmation. Competitive pricing requires 2+ independent sources. Company-listed prices = 1 point only.
  • Opportunity valuation: STR-003 (Idea Development) + RSC-009. Each value driver needs traceable data. If data is poisoned, the valuation is invalid.
  • Competitive intelligence: RSC-004 + RSC-009. Company claims vs. independent verification. Marketing materials = 1 confirmation point, not 2.
  • Clinical/regulatory claims: RSC-004 (with primary source requirement). 3 confirmation points minimum. Primary registry (FDA, CE, ClinicalTrials.gov) always required.
  • Financial projections: RSC-009 (assumption transparency). Every assumption listed. Sensitivity analysis on poisoned-source-dependent assumptions.

Rule: No evaluation output without both (1) the correct methodology protocol activated and (2) EPS-002 confirmation applied to every data point.

If you're about to produce a market sizing, pricing analysis, or opportunity assessment and you haven't explicitly named which protocol you're following — STOP. Name it. Follow it. Then apply EPS-002 on top.

Application to NotebookLM Extractions

The knowledge extracted from NotebookLM on 2026-08-07 (381 YouTube sources across 7 notebooks) captures what was said in those videos, not independently verified facts. Specific tactical claims (formulas, metrics, tools, techniques) should be treated as:

  • Confirmed at level 5 (single source with medium provenance — YouTube creator authority varies)
  • Requires independent confirmation before professional use
  • Useful for direction and strategy, NOT for citing specific numbers in investor-facing or clinical documents

The extraction IS valuable for: frameworks, system architectures, tool stacks, workflow patterns, content strategies, and tactical approaches. It is NOT reliable for: specific statistics, revenue claims, conversion rates, or market sizing without separate confirmation.

Interaction with EPS-001

This protocol extends EPS-001 (Epistemic Standards). Where EPS-001 defines claim metadata format and confidence levels, EPS-002 adds:

  • Minimum confirmation point requirements
  • Source contamination rules (total discard)
  • Provenance tracking (poisoned: field)
  • Special scrutiny domains
  • Ranked confirmation methods
  • Mandatory methodology cross-reference
  • NotebookLM extraction guidance

Both protocols are active. EPS-002 does not replace EPS-001 — it strengthens it.

Quick Reference: When You're About to State a Fact

  1. Where did this data come from?
  1. Can I confirm it from an independent source?
  1. Does it fall in a special scrutiny domain?
  1. If the source were poisoned, would I still be able to make this claim?
  1. Am I about to use NotebookLM extraction data as verified fact? → STOP. Tag it. Confirm it first.
  1. Am I about to produce an evaluation, pricing, or market sizing without activating the correct methodology protocol? → STOP. Name it. Follow it.
