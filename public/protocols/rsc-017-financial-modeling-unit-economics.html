RSC-017: Financial Modeling & Unit Economics

Department: RSC (Research) Version: 1.0 Created: 2026-06-30 Owner: Lumina Status: ACTIVE Source: Roo research prompt library — VP of Finance at high-growth startup

Purpose

Complete unit economics breakdown and 3-year financial projection with CAC, LTV, break-even analysis, cash flow forecast, sensitivity analysis, and industry benchmarking.

Trigger

  • When Roo asks for financial model, unit economics, or revenue projection
  • When evaluating business viability or preparing for fundraising
  • When needing to understand profitability drivers

    • Role

    You are a VP of Finance at a high-growth startup.

    Inputs Required (fill at activation)

  • BUSINESS MODEL: [How the company makes money]
  • CURRENT REVENUE: [If any]
  • COSTS: [Fixed and variable cost structure]
  • GROWTH RATE: [Current growth trajectory]

    • Output Structure

    1. Unit Economics

  • CAC by channel (Customer Acquisition Cost)
  • LTV calculation with assumptions (Lifetime Value)
  • LTV:CAC ratio and payback period
  • Gross margin per unit/customer
  • Contribution margin analysis

    • 2. 3-Year Financial Projection

  • Revenue model: Monthly for Year 1, Quarterly for Years 2-3
  • Cost structure: Fixed vs. variable breakdown
  • Break-even analysis: When and at what volume
  • Cash flow forecast with burn rate

    • 3. Sensitivity Analysis

  • Best case, Base case, Worst case
  • What variables drive each scenario

    • 4. Key Assumptions Table

  • Every assumption with justification
  • Impact rating: which assumptions matter most

    • 5. Benchmark Comparison

  • How metrics compare to industry standards
  • CAC, LTV, gross margin, growth rate vs. industry medians

    • 6. Red Flags

  • What numbers should trigger action
  • Thresholds for concern

    • Format

    Financial model summary with clear tables and formulas.

    Epistemic Standards

  • Every financial figure: [source: actual/estimated/projected] [confidence: H/M/L]
  • Assumptions explicitly listed — never hide assumptions in a number
  • If industry benchmarks unavailable, say so rather than fabricating
