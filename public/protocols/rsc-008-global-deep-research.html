RSC-008 Global Deep Research Protocol

Department: RSC (Research) Version: 1.0 Created: 2026-06-18 Owner: Lumina Status: ACTIVE Trigger: Any research project spanning multiple phases, regions, and depth levels requiring a structured evidence base for strategic decision-making

---

Purpose

This protocol governs large-scale, multi-phase research projects that produce foundational evidence bases for long-range strategic decisions. It is designed for projects that:

  • Span multiple geographic regions
  • Require multiple depth levels simultaneously
  • Need a reusable, searchable evidence repository
  • Feed into future-state analysis (Phase 2)
  • Must be defensible, source-referenced, and commercially relevant

    • This is NOT for quick lookups, single-source answers, or brief summaries. Use RSC-001 for those.

    ---

    Internal Quality Rubric

    Every section of the research must score HIGH on all ten dimensions before the dossier is considered complete:

    1. Source Quality — Tier 1 sources (peer-reviewed journals, registries, WHO data) preferred. Tier 3 sources (market reports, white papers) only where Tier 1/2 unavailable. Every claim traced to its source. 2. Evidence Hierarchy — Systematic reviews > RCTs > Cohort studies > Case series > Expert opinion > Market reports. Hierarchy explicitly noted. 3. Data Validation — Cross-referenced across at least 2 sources where possible. Conflicting evidence presented with both sides. No fabrication. Unknowns labeled as UNKNOWN, not estimated. 4. Regional Completeness — Every required region covered at minimum Level 1-2. Priority regions (specified per project) covered at all 7 levels. Gaps explicitly noted per region. 5. Strategic Relevance — Every finding answers "What does this mean for device development?" Unmet needs, emerging technologies, and regional differences highlighted. 6. Forecasting Rigor — Future signals labeled as WEAK SIGNAL, EARLY INDICATOR, or ESTABLISHED TREND. No claims about the future presented as certainty. Time horizons specified. 7. Clinical Accuracy — All clinical claims verified against guideline sources (AHA/ASA, ESO, WSO, JNIS). Procedure names, device names, and clinical terminology used correctly. 8. Engineering Applicability — Device landscape includes specific products, manufacturers, and technical specifications. Adoption patterns include numbers where available. Preference shifts documented with evidence. 9. Commercial Relevance — Market sizes tagged [VERIFIED] or [ESTIMATE]. Reimbursement codes included where applicable. Hospital incentives and funding mechanisms documented. 10. Actionability — Every section ends with strategic implications. The dossier enables decision-making, not just information delivery.

    ---

    Research Depth Levels

    Every research project under this protocol operates at multiple depth levels simultaneously:

    | Level | Name | Focus | Key Questions | |-------|------|-------|----------------| | 1 | Executive Landscape | Incidence, prevalence, mortality, disability, economic burden | How big is the problem? | | 2 | Healthcare Systems | Infrastructure, centers, EMS, referral pathways, access | How does care get delivered? | | 3 | Treatment Landscape | Therapies, procedures, approaches, outcomes | What treatments exist and how are they used? | | 4 | Device Landscape | Products, manufacturers, market share, adoption, preferences | What devices are used and by whom? | | 5 | Clinical Trends | Guideline evolution, procedure growth, trial influence, training | How is practice changing? | | 6 | Economic Trends | Reimbursement, funding, incentives, cost-effectiveness, barriers | What drives or blocks adoption? | | 7 | Future Signals | Weak signals, early indicators, procedural shifts, tech, care models | What's coming next? |

    ---

    Source Tiers

    | Tier | Sources | Use For | |------|---------|---------| | 1 | PubMed, JAMA, Stroke, Lancet, NEJM, Nature, Circulation, JNIS, European Stroke Journal, AHA/ASA guidelines, ESO guidelines, WSO, SVIN, SNIS | Clinical evidence, epidemiology, outcomes data | | 2 | National Stroke Registries, WHO, OECD, CDC, NHS, SSNAP, GWTG-Stroke, German Stroke Registry, Chinese Stroke Association, Japanese Stroke Society, government health reports | Regional data, system performance, infrastructure | | 3 | Investor presentations, market reports (Grand View, Markets and Markets, Allied), conference proceedings, white papers, manufacturer annual reports | Market sizing, device landscape, commercial context |

    Rule: Every major claim must cite at least one Tier 1 or Tier 2 source. Tier 3 sources supplement but do not replace Tier 1/2.

    ---

    Literature Collection Standard

    For every source collected:

    

    Source Entry
    ID: [unique identifier, e.g., SRC-001]
Category: [Epidemiology / Treatment / Device / Economic / Guideline / Market / Future]
Region: [Global / US / EU / Japan / China / APAC / etc.]
Tier: [1 / 2 / 3]
Title: [full title]
Authors: [first author et al., year]
Publication: [journal name]
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]
DOI: [doi if available]
URL: [link to source]
Access: [Open Access / Paywall / Abstract Only / Full Text Available]
Evidence Strength: [Systematic Review / RCT / Cohort / Case Series / Expert Opinion / Market Report]
Confidence: [HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW]
Key Takeaway: [1-2 sentences]
Strategic Relevance: [1-2 sentences — what this means for device development]

    Minimum target: 50-100 high-quality sources for a full Phase 1 research project.

    ---

    Research Execution Phases

    Phase 1: Global Landscape Research (Current Protocol)

    1. Define scope — regions, depth levels, timeline, strategic lens 2. Build search framework — key terms, MeSH terms, search strings per region per level 3. Execute systematic search — query all tiers, collect and catalog sources 4. Extract and tag — pull claims, assign confidence, tag region/level/category 5. Cross-reference — validate claims across sources, flag conflicts 6. Regional analysis — write each region section with all 7 levels (where data exists) 7. Global synthesis — executive summary, device landscape, knowledge gaps 8. Phase 2 preparation — define key questions for future-state analysis 9. Quality review — score against rubric, refine until all sections score HIGH

    Phase 2: Future State Analysis (Separate Protocol)

    Phase 2 is ONLY informed by Phase 1 findings. It does NOT begin until Phase 1 is complete and reviewed by the stakeholder. Phase 2 has its own protocol (to be written when Phase 1 is complete).

    ---

    Session Management

    For projects requiring multiple sessions (most will):

    1. Create a project file — memory/research/[project-name]/ directory 2. Checkpoint after every session — write progress to memory/research/[project-name]/progress.md 3. Source database — maintain memory/research/[project-name]/sources.md as a growing evidence repository 4. Use context rollover — when context fills past 80%, create checkpoint via scripts/context-rollover.py 5. Regenerate COMPASS — after significant progress, run python scripts/compass-generator.py to update session orientation

    ---

    Quality Gates (Before Declaring Any Section Complete)

    Every claim has a source attribution (SRC-XXX format)
    Every source has a complete entry in the literature database
    Conflicting evidence is presented with both sides
    No fabricated data — use UNKNOWN or [0.1 MARKER: no data found]
    Confidence levels assigned to every major claim
    Evidence, inference, and speculation are clearly separated
    Strategic implications stated for every section
    Regional gaps explicitly noted (not assumed)
    All 7 depth levels addressed (or gaps noted)
    Source count meets minimum (50-100)

    ---

    Error Handling

    | Error | Response | |-------|----------| | Tier 1 source contradicts Tier 3 | Present Tier 1 as primary, note Tier 3 contradiction | | No data exists for a region | Write "NO DATA FOUND" — do NOT estimate from adjacent regions | | Paywall blocks full text | Use abstract + existing summaries, note access limitation | | Source provides conflicting numbers | Present both, note methodology differences, assign MEDIUM confidence | | Clinical terminology uncertain | Use WHO/ICD terminology, cross-reference guideline sources | | Market data only from company reports | Tag as [ESTIMATE: company-reported], note potential bias |

    ---

    Output Format

    Section A: Global Executive Summary

  • Current state of stroke globally
  • Most important findings
  • Strategic implications

    • Section B: Regional Landscape Analysis

  • Separate sections per region
  • Population burden, treatment adoption, infrastructure, technology, challenges, future outlook

    • Section C: Global Device Landscape

  • Current devices, regional preferences, market leaders, technology trends, clinical adoption

    • Section D: Literature Database

  • Structured evidence repository with all source entries
  • Minimum 50-100 high-quality sources

    • Section E: Knowledge Gaps

  • Unknowns, data limitations, research gaps, regional blind spots

    • Section F: Phase 2 Preparation

  • Key questions for "Future State" analysis
  • Do NOT perform Phase 2

    • ---

    Deliverable Standard

    The final dossier must be:

  • Evidence-based — every claim traced to a source
  • Source-referenced — SRC-XXX format throughout
  • Defensible — confidence levels on every major claim
  • Executive-ready — clear structure, actionable insights
  • R&D relevant — answers "what does this mean for device development?"
  • Commercially relevant — market sizes, reimbursement, adoption barriers
  • Clinically accurate — correct terminology, guideline references
  • Globally informed — all specified regions covered (gaps noted where data unavailable)
